# Adverbes de liaison

Les **adverbes de liaison** relient deux phrases. Contrairement aux conjonctions, ils ne relient pas deux propositions dans la même phrase : ils sont suivis d'une virgule et précédés d'un point ou d'un point-virgule.

## Par fonction

| Fonction | Adverbes | Exemple |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Opposition** | however, nevertheless, still, on the other hand | *The offer is generous; **however**, it expires soon.* |
| **Conséquence** | therefore, consequently, as a result, thus | *Demand rose; **therefore**, prices increased.* |
| **Addition** | moreover, furthermore, in addition, also | *The room is large; **moreover**, it is quiet.* |
| **Condition / alternative** | otherwise, instead, alternatively | *Sign today; **otherwise**, the price will change.* |
| **Temps** | meanwhile, afterward, then, subsequently | *The manager left. **Meanwhile**, the staff continued.* |
| **Exemple** | for instance, for example | *Many perks, **for instance**, gym access.* |

## Adverbe ou conjonction ?

- Conjonction : *Although* it was late, we stayed. (même phrase, pas de virgule avant)
- Adverbe : *It was late. **However**, we stayed.* (deux phrases)

## Part 6 : l'astuce

Dans les textes à compléter, lisez **la phrase d'avant** : le mot de liaison dépend du rapport logique (opposition, conséquence, addition), pas de la grammaire.

## Pièges des francophones

- *However* n'est jamais une conjonction : pas de « *It was cheap however it broke* » sans ponctuation.
- *Therefore* ≠ *therefor*. *Otherwise* = « sinon », pas « autrement dit ».

# Linking adverbs

**Linking adverbs** connect two sentences. Unlike conjunctions, they do not join two clauses within one sentence: they follow a period or a semicolon and are followed by a comma.

## By function

| Function | Adverbs | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Contrast** | however, nevertheless, still, on the other hand | *The offer is generous; **however**, it expires soon.* |
| **Result** | therefore, consequently, as a result, thus | *Demand rose; **therefore**, prices increased.* |
| **Addition** | moreover, furthermore, in addition, also | *The room is large; **moreover**, it is quiet.* |
| **Condition / alternative** | otherwise, instead, alternatively | *Sign today; **otherwise**, the price will change.* |
| **Time** | meanwhile, afterward, then, subsequently | *The manager left. **Meanwhile**, the staff continued.* |
| **Example** | for instance, for example | *Many perks, **for instance**, gym access.* |

## Adverb or conjunction?

- Conjunction: *Although* it was late, we stayed. (same sentence, no comma before)
- Adverb: *It was late. **However**, we stayed.* (two sentences)

## Part 6 tip

In text-completion passages, read **the sentence before**: the linking word depends on the logical relationship (contrast, result, addition), not on grammar.

## Traps for French speakers

- *However* is never a conjunction: no run-on like "It was cheap however it broke".
- *Otherwise* = "or else", not "in other words".
