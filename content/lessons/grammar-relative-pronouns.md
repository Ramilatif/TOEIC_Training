# Les pronoms relatifs

Ils relient une **proposition relative** à un nom (l'antécédent). Choisissez selon **l'antécédent** et **la fonction** dans la relative.

## Tableau

| Pronom | Antécédent | Fonction | Exemple |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **who** | personne | sujet | The manager **who** called is here. |
| **whom** | personne | complément | The manager **whom** we hired is here. |
| **which** | chose | sujet / complément | The report **which** arrived is long. |
| **that** | personne ou chose | sujet / complément (sans virgule) | The report **that** I wrote is ready. |
| **whose** | personne ou chose | possession | The firm **whose** CEO resigned is small. |
| **where** | lieu | lieu | The office **where** we met is closed. |
| **when** | temps | temps | The day **when** we signed was Monday. |

## Règles pratiques

1. **Sujet** ou **complément** ? Si un sujet suit le pronom (*whom **we** hired*), c'est un complément.
2. **Whose** est toujours suivi d'un **nom**.
3. **Which** (pas *that*) après une virgule ou une préposition : *the room **in which** we met*.
4. **Pas de pronom** doublon : *The man who he called…* est faux.

<!-- extra -->
## Pièges des francophones

- **who** = personne, **which** = chose, **that** = personne ou chose (sans virgule). *The employee **who** called…* / *The device **which** failed…*
- **Pas de « that » après une virgule** ni après une préposition : *the manager, **who** was late* ; *the company **for which** he works*.
- **whose** = dont (possession) : *a company **whose** profits rose*.
- **Pas de pronom doublé** : *The report that I wrote it* est faux. *The report **that** I wrote…*
- **« dont » ne se traduit pas toujours par whose** : *the project I told you **about*** (préposition rejetée à la fin).

## Exemples en contexte

- *Anyone **who** wishes to attend must register.*
- *The software, **which** was released in May, has many users.*

# Relative pronouns

They link a **relative clause** to a noun (the antecedent). Choose according to the **antecedent** and the **function** inside the clause.

## Table

| Pronoun | Antecedent | Function | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **who** | person | subject | The manager **who** called is here. |
| **whom** | person | object | The manager **whom** we hired is here. |
| **which** | thing | subject / object | The report **which** arrived is long. |
| **that** | person or thing | subject / object (no comma) | The report **that** I wrote is ready. |
| **whose** | person or thing | possession | The firm **whose** CEO resigned is small. |
| **where** | place | place | The office **where** we met is closed. |
| **when** | time | time | The day **when** we signed was Monday. |

## Practical rules

1. **Subject** or **object**? If a subject follows the pronoun (*whom **we** hired*), it is an object.
2. **Whose** is always followed by a **noun**.
3. **Which** (not *that*) after a comma or a preposition: *the room **in which** we met*.
4. **No duplicate pronoun**: *The man who he called…* is wrong.

## Traps for French speakers

- **who** = person, **which** = thing, **that** = person or thing (no comma). *The employee **who** called…* / *The device **which** failed…*
- **No "that" after a comma** or after a preposition: *the manager, **who** was late*; *the company **for which** he works*.
- **whose** = of whom/which (possession): *a company **whose** profits rose*.
- **No doubled pronoun**: "The report that I wrote it" is wrong. *The report **that** I wrote…*
- **French "dont" is not always whose**: *the project I told you **about*** (preposition at the end).

## Examples in context

- *Anyone **who** wishes to attend must register.*
- *The software, **which** was released in May, has many users.*
