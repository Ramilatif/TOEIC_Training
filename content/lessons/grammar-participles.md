# Les participes

Deux formes : le **participe présent (-ing)** et le **participe passé (-ed / irrégulier)**. Ils servent d'adjectifs et de raccourcis de propositions.

## Adjectif en -ing ou en -ed ?

| Forme | Sens | Exemple |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **-ing** | ce qui **provoque** l'effet (actif) | The results were **surprising**. |
| **-ed** | celui qui **ressent** l'effet (passif) | We were **surprised** by the results. |

Règle rapide : une **personne** qui ressent → *-ed* ; une **chose** qui provoque → *-ing*.

## Participe passé = sens passif

*the **required** documents* (les documents qui sont requis)
*a **damaged** package*, *an **experienced** manager*.

## Participe présent = sens actif

*the **leading** company*, *an **increasing** number*, *a **growing** demand*.

## Propositions participiales

- ***Having reviewed** the file, she approved it.* (action antérieure)
- ***Reading** the contract, he found an error.* (même sujet obligatoire)
- ***Located** downtown, the hotel is convenient.* (passif : le lieu **est** situé)

## Piège

Le sujet de la proposition participiale doit être **le même** que celui de la principale : *Walking home, **the rain** started* est faux.

<!-- lang:en -->
# Participles

Two forms: the **present participle (-ing)** and the **past participle (-ed / irregular)**. They work as adjectives and as shortcuts for clauses.

## -ing or -ed adjective?

| Form | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **-ing** | what **causes** the effect (active) | The results were **surprising**. |
| **-ed** | who **feels** the effect (passive) | We were **surprised** by the results. |

Quick rule: a **person** who feels → *-ed*; a **thing** that causes → *-ing*.

## Past participle = passive meaning

*the **required** documents* (documents that are required)
*a **damaged** package*, *an **experienced** manager*.

## Present participle = active meaning

*the **leading** company*, *an **increasing** number*, *a **growing** demand*.

## Participial clauses

- ***Having reviewed** the file, she approved it.* (earlier action)
- ***Reading** the contract, he found an error.* (same subject required)
- ***Located** downtown, the hotel is convenient.* (passive: the place **is** located)

## Trap

The subject of the participial clause must be **the same** as the main clause: *Walking home, **the rain** started* is wrong.
