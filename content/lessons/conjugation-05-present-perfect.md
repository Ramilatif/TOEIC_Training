# Le present perfect

## Formation

**have / has + participe passé**

| Forme | Exemple |
| --- | --- |
| Affirmatif | I / you / we / they **have finished** · he / she / it **has finished** |
| Négatif | *We **have not (haven't)** received it.* · *She **has not (hasn't)** called.* |
| Question | ***Have** you finished?* · ***Has** he arrived?* |
| Progressif | *I **have been working** here for two years.* (have/has **been** + -ing) |

## Quand l'employer ?

| Emploi | Exemple | Indices |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Lien avec le présent** : résultat visible maintenant | *The price **has increased**.* | just, already, yet |
| **Période non terminée** | *We **have sold** 500 units this month.* | this week/month/year, so far, recently |
| **Durée jusqu'à maintenant** | *She **has worked** here **since** 2019.* | since, for, how long |
| **Expérience** | *I **have visited** Tokyo twice.* | ever, never, before, twice |

## since et for

- **since** + point de départ : *since 2020, since Monday, since the merger*
- **for** + durée : *for five years, for two weeks, for a long time*

## Present perfect ou prétérit ?

| Present perfect | Prétérit |
| --- | --- |
| *I **have lost** my badge.* (je ne l'ai toujours pas) | *I **lost** my badge **yesterday**.* |
| *We **have met** twice.* | *We **met** in May.* |

**Règle** : une date ou un marqueur de passé précis (*yesterday, last week, in 2021, ago*) exige le **prétérit**.

## Pièges des francophones

- **« Je travaille ici depuis 2 ans »** → *I **have worked** here for two years* (jamais *I work … since*).
- **« Il est arrivé hier »** → *He **arrived** yesterday* (le français dit « il est arrivé », l'anglais refuse le present perfect avec *yesterday*).
- **Participes irréguliers** : *seen, written, given, taken, begun* (voir la leçon sur les verbes irréguliers).
- ***been* ≠ *gone*** : *She **has been** to Paris* (elle y est allée et revenue) ≠ *She **has gone** to Paris* (elle y est encore).

# The present perfect

## Formation

**have / has + past participle**

| Form | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Affirmative | I / you / we / they **have finished** · he / she / it **has finished** |
| Negative | *We **have not (haven't)** received it.* · *She **has not (hasn't)** called.* |
| Question | ***Have** you finished?* · ***Has** he arrived?* |
| Progressive | *I **have been working** here for two years.* (have/has **been** + -ing) |

## When to use it

| Use | Example | Clues |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Link with the present**: visible result now | *The price **has increased**.* | just, already, yet |
| **Unfinished period** | *We **have sold** 500 units this month.* | this week/month/year, so far, recently |
| **Duration up to now** | *She **has worked** here **since** 2019.* | since, for, how long |
| **Experience** | *I **have visited** Tokyo twice.* | ever, never, before, twice |

## since and for

- **since** + starting point: *since 2020, since Monday, since the merger*
- **for** + length: *for five years, for two weeks, for a long time*

## Present perfect or simple past?

| Present perfect | Simple past |
| --- | --- |
| *I **have lost** my badge.* (I still do not have it) | *I **lost** my badge **yesterday**.* |
| *We **have met** twice.* | *We **met** in May.* |

**Rule**: a date or a precise past marker (*yesterday, last week, in 2021, ago*) requires the **simple past**.

## Traps for French speakers

- **"Je travaille ici depuis 2 ans"** → *I **have worked** here for two years* (never *I work … since*).
- **"Il est arrivé hier"** → *He **arrived** yesterday* (French uses a compound past; English refuses the present perfect with *yesterday*).
- **Irregular participles**: *seen, written, given, taken, begun* (see the irregular verbs lesson).
- ***been* ≠ *gone***: *She **has been** to Paris* (went and came back) ≠ *She **has gone** to Paris* (still there).
