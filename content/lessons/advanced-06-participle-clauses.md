# Propositions participiales et subordonnées réduites

Une **proposition participiale** remplace une subordonnée entière par un participe. Elle rend le style plus dense, typique des textes de niveau C1.

## 1. Les trois formes

| Forme | Sens | Exemple |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Participe présent** (-ing) | action simultanée ou cause | ***Working** late, she missed the call.* (= *While she was working late…*) |
| **Participe passé** (-ed) | sens passif | ***Written** in 1995, the report is outdated.* (= *Because it was written…*) |
| **Having + participe passé** | action antérieure | ***Having reviewed** the contract, we signed it.* (= *After we had reviewed…*) |
| **Being + participe passé** (passif progressif) | passif simultané | ***Being repaired**, the machine is unavailable.* |
| **Not + participe** | négation | ***Not knowing** the answer, he asked a colleague.* |

## 2. Règle d'or : le même sujet

Le sujet **implicite** du participe doit être le **sujet de la proposition principale**.

- ✔ ***Having finished** the report, **Ms. Cho** left.* (Ms. Cho a fini et est partie.)
- ✘ ***Having finished** the report, **the door** was closed.* (la porte n'a pas fini le rapport — « dangling participle »)

## 3. Participes figés (sujet différent autorisé)

*given that, provided (that), providing, considering, judging by, based on, speaking of, assuming (that), granted that*.

- ***Given that** prices are rising, we must act.*
- ***Considering** the delay, the result is impressive.*

## 4. Participe après un nom (proposition relative réduite)

| Relative complète | Réduite |
| --- | --- |
| *the employees **who work** here* | *the employees **working** here* |
| *the report **that was written** by Ms. Cho* | *the report **written** by Ms. Cho* |
| *the people **who were invited*** | *the people **invited*** |

## 5. Participe avec conjonction

*Before **leaving**, please sign.* · *After **reviewing** the file, call me.* · *While **waiting**, read the brochure.* · *Although **tired**, he finished.*

## Pièges

- **Dangling participle** : vérifiez toujours que le sujet logique est celui de la principale.
- **-ing vs -ed** : *The **boring** presentation* (qui ennuie) ≠ *the **bored** audience* (qui s'ennuie).
- **Antériorité** : si l'action participiale est **avant** l'action principale, utilisez *having + participe passé*.

<!-- lang:en -->
# Participle clauses and reduced clauses

A **participle clause** replaces a whole subordinate clause with a participle. It makes the style denser, typical of C1-level texts.

## 1. The three forms

| Form | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Present participle** (-ing) | simultaneous action or cause | ***Working** late, she missed the call.* (= *While she was working late…*) |
| **Past participle** (-ed) | passive meaning | ***Written** in 1995, the report is outdated.* (= *Because it was written…*) |
| **Having + past participle** | earlier action | ***Having reviewed** the contract, we signed it.* (= *After we had reviewed…*) |
| **Being + past participle** (progressive passive) | simultaneous passive | ***Being repaired**, the machine is unavailable.* |
| **Not + participle** | negative | ***Not knowing** the answer, he asked a colleague.* |

## 2. Golden rule: same subject

The **implied** subject of the participle must be the **subject of the main clause**.

- ✔ ***Having finished** the report, **Ms. Cho** left.* (Ms. Cho finished and left.)
- ✘ ***Having finished** the report, **the door** was closed.* (the door did not finish the report — "dangling participle")

## 3. Fixed participles (different subject allowed)

*given that, provided (that), providing, considering, judging by, based on, speaking of, assuming (that), granted that*.

- ***Given that** prices are rising, we must act.*
- ***Considering** the delay, the result is impressive.*

## 4. Participle after a noun (reduced relative clause)

| Full relative clause | Reduced |
| --- | --- |
| *the employees **who work** here* | *the employees **working** here* |
| *the report **that was written** by Ms. Cho* | *the report **written** by Ms. Cho* |
| *the people **who were invited*** | *the people **invited*** |

## 5. Participle with a conjunction

*Before **leaving**, please sign.* · *After **reviewing** the file, call me.* · *While **waiting**, read the brochure.* · *Although **tired**, he finished.*

## Traps

- **Dangling participle**: always check that the logical subject is that of the main clause.
- **-ing vs -ed**: *The **boring** presentation* (causes boredom) ≠ *the **bored** audience* (feels boredom).
- **Earlier action**: if the participle action comes **before** the main action, use *having + past participle*.
