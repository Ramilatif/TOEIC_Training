# Discours rapporté et inversion

## Discours rapporté

On « recule » le temps d'un cran quand le verbe introducteur est au passé.

| Discours direct | Discours rapporté |
| --- | --- |
| "I **am** busy." | He said he **was** busy. |
| "I **will** call." | She said she **would** call. |
| "We **have finished**." | They said they **had finished**. |
| "Can you help?" | He asked **if** I could help. |
| "Where is the file?" | She asked **where the file was**. |

Changements de repères : *today → that day*, *tomorrow → the next day*, *now → then*, *here → there*.

**Verbes de rapport** : *say* (sans complément personne), *tell + personne*, *ask*, *explain*, *announce*, *inform + personne + that*, *remind + personne + to*.

## Inversion

L'inversion sujet-auxiliaire apparaît après certains mots négatifs ou restrictifs en tête de phrase :

- ***Never / Rarely / Seldom** have we seen such growth.*
- ***Not only** did the team finish early, **but** it also saved money.*
- ***Hardly** had we begun **when** the alarm rang.*
- ***Only after** the audit **did** we find the error.*

## Piège

Dans une question rapportée, **plus d'inversion** : *She asked where **the file was**.* (et non *where was the file*).

<!-- extra -->
## Pièges des francophones

- **Concordance des temps** : si le verbe introducteur est au passé, on « recule » d'un temps. *"I **am** busy"* → *He said he **was** busy.*
- **Pas de ponctuation ni d'inversion** dans le discours rapporté : *She asked **where the office was*** (pas *where was the office*).
- **say vs tell** : *say (that)…*, *tell + personne + (that)…* : *He **told me** he was late.*
- **Questions indirectes** : *if / whether* pour oui/non.
- **Inversion avec adverbe négatif** (TOEIC) : *Rarely **do we** see…*, *Not only **did he** arrive late…*

## Exemples en contexte

- *The manager said that the report **would be** ready by noon.*
- *He asked **whether** the meeting had been cancelled.*

# Reported speech and inversion

## Reported speech

Backshift the tense when the reporting verb is in the past.

| Direct speech | Reported speech |
| --- | --- |
| "I **am** busy." | He said he **was** busy. |
| "I **will** call." | She said she **would** call. |
| "We **have finished**." | They said they **had finished**. |
| "Can you help?" | He asked **if** I could help. |
| "Where is the file?" | She asked **where the file was**. |

Time shifts: *today → that day*, *tomorrow → the next day*, *now → then*, *here → there*.

**Reporting verbs**: *say* (no person object), *tell + person*, *ask*, *explain*, *announce*, *inform + person + that*, *remind + person + to*.

## Inversion

Subject-auxiliary inversion appears after some negative or restrictive words at the start of a sentence:

- ***Never / Rarely / Seldom** have we seen such growth.*
- ***Not only** did the team finish early, **but** it also saved money.*
- ***Hardly** had we begun **when** the alarm rang.*
- ***Only after** the audit **did** we find the error.*

## Trap

In a reported question there is **no inversion**: *She asked where **the file was**.* (not *where was the file*).

## Traps for French speakers

- **Backshift**: if the reporting verb is in the past, tenses move back. *"I **am** busy"* → *He said he **was** busy.*
- **No question inversion** in reported speech: *She asked **where the office was*** (not "where was the office").
- **say vs tell**: *say (that)…*, *tell + person + (that)…*: *He **told me** he was late.*
- **Indirect questions**: *if / whether* for yes/no.
- **Inversion after negative adverbs** (TOEIC): *Rarely **do we** see…*, *Not only **did he** arrive late…*

## Examples in context

- *The manager said that the report **would be** ready by noon.*
- *He asked **whether** the meeting had been cancelled.*
