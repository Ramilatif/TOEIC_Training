# Les phrases clivées

Une **phrase clivée** découpe une phrase simple en deux parties pour **mettre en relief** un élément. C'est typique du style C1, à l'écrit comme à l'oral.

## 1. It-cleft : It is / was + élément + that / who

Phrase de base : *The delay annoyed the client.*

| Élément mis en relief | Phrase clivée |
| --- | --- |
| le sujet | ***It was the delay that** annoyed the client.* |
| l'objet | ***It was the client that** the delay annoyed.* |
| le moment | ***It was last week that** we signed the contract.* |
| le lieu | ***It is in Paris that** the meeting will take place.* |
| une personne | ***It was Ms. Reed who** approved the plan.* |

**Règle** : *who* pour une personne, *that* sinon. Le verbe *be* est au temps du récit (*is* ou *was*).

## 2. Wh-cleft (pseudo-cleft) : What … is / was + élément

| Phrase simple | Phrase clivée |
| --- | --- |
| *We need more staff.* | ***What we need is** more staff.* |
| *I disliked the noise.* | ***What I disliked was** the noise.* |
| *They decided to postpone.* | ***What they decided to do was** (to) postpone.* |

Variantes : ***The reason why** we left was…* · ***The place where** we met was…* · ***The thing that** matters is…* · ***All that** remains is…*

## 3. Autres procédés de mise en relief

- **Do emphatique** : *I **do** understand your concern.* / *We **did** send the invoice.*
- **Renforcement** : *very, quite, absolutely, utterly* + adjectif extrême (*absolutely essential*).
- **Mise en tête** : *Most important of all is the budget.*

## Pièges

- **Accord** : le verbe *be* suit le groupe clivé : *What we need **is** staff* / *What we need **are** two engineers* (le pluriel est possible en style soutenu).
- **Pas de double sujet** : *It was Ms. Reed **who she** approved…* est faux.
- **« C'est… que »** se rend par *It is… that* ; ne traduisez pas par *This is… that*.
- **Temps** : *It was in 2019 that we **founded** the firm* (le verbe après *that* suit le sens, ici le passé).

# Cleft sentences

A **cleft sentence** splits a simple sentence in two to **emphasize** one element. It is typical of C1-level style, both written and spoken.

## 1. It-cleft: It is / was + element + that / who

Base sentence: *The delay annoyed the client.*

| Emphasized element | Cleft sentence |
| --- | --- |
| the subject | ***It was the delay that** annoyed the client.* |
| the object | ***It was the client that** the delay annoyed.* |
| the time | ***It was last week that** we signed the contract.* |
| the place | ***It is in Paris that** the meeting will take place.* |
| a person | ***It was Ms. Reed who** approved the plan.* |

**Rule**: *who* for a person, *that* otherwise. The verb *be* follows the narrative tense (*is* or *was*).

## 2. Wh-cleft (pseudo-cleft): What … is / was + element

| Simple sentence | Cleft sentence |
| --- | --- |
| *We need more staff.* | ***What we need is** more staff.* |
| *I disliked the noise.* | ***What I disliked was** the noise.* |
| *They decided to postpone.* | ***What they decided to do was** (to) postpone.* |

Variants: ***The reason why** we left was…* · ***The place where** we met was…* · ***The thing that** matters is…* · ***All that** remains is…*

## 3. Other ways to emphasize

- **Emphatic do**: *I **do** understand your concern.* / *We **did** send the invoice.*
- **Intensifiers**: *very, quite, absolutely, utterly* + extreme adjective (*absolutely essential*).
- **Fronting**: *Most important of all is the budget.*

## Traps

- **Agreement**: *be* follows the cleft group: *What we need **is** staff* / *What we need **are** two engineers* (the plural is possible in formal style).
- **No doubled subject**: "It was Ms. Reed **who she** approved…" is wrong.
- **French "c'est… que"** becomes *It is… that*; do not translate with *This is… that*.
- **Tense**: *It was in 2019 that we **founded** the firm* (the verb after *that* follows the meaning, here the past).
